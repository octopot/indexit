# v0.2.1 — Know your role.

`telegram fetch dialogs` now includes your account's membership, admin, and owner status. Find channels and groups you belong to but do not administer using the dialog list alone, without an extra request for each chat. `telegram fetch peer` includes the same status in channel and group cards.

## Find your ordinary memberships

```sh
indexit -q telegram fetch dialogs \
  | jq -c 'select(.peer_type | IN("channel", "supergroup", "chat")) | select(.is_member == true and .is_admin == false)'
```

- `is_member` tells you whether your signed-in account currently belongs to the chat. Groups that have been replaced by a supergroup are not counted as current memberships.
- `is_admin` includes both administrators and owners. `is_creator` identifies ownership separately.
- The fields work for channels, supergroups, and basic groups. Remove `"chat"` from the filter if you only want channels and supergroups.
- These are your account's rights; names and member counts are not used to infer who owns a chat.

[Filter your dialogs →](/guide/explore/#find-chats-where-you-are-an-ordinary-member)

## Keep unknown status distinct

- Known values are explicit `true` or `false`. When Telegram supplies too little information, the status fields are absent. Keep those records for separate review instead of treating them as non-admin memberships.
- Channel direct-message inboxes also leave this status absent; they are not ordinary group memberships.
- Telegram may retain ownership flags on a channel you left or a group that was replaced by a supergroup. Use `is_member == true` together with `is_creator == true` to find owned chats you currently belong to.
